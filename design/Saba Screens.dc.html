RTL notes — beyond mirroring
The countdown stays LTR. 02:14:09 is a clock, not a sentence. It keeps its direction inside an RTL row, otherwise the hours and seconds swap places.
Discount badges stay LTR. “−25%” with the minus leading; in an RTL box the sign would otherwise render after the number.
Carousels scroll from the right and the first card sits against the right gutter. The section's trailing padding moves with it, so the last card still peeks.
Arabic titles need the taller line height. Card titles run to two lines more often, so the card body is sized for two lines in both languages and never reflows between them.
The notification dot and the wishlist heart swap corners — the dot sits on the icon's trailing-top, which is the left side in Arabic.
Prices keep Western digits, only the currency mark changes to د.ع, and it trails the number in both directions.

Product detail — decisions and RTL notes
The nav hides here. The sticky buy bar replaces it, so there is only ever one floating element at the bottom. The bar is a rounded sheet edge, not a pill, which tells you it belongs to the page and not to the app.
The rating pill straddles the seam between the image stage and the white body — it is the hinge between looking and deciding, and it is tappable straight through to reviews.
Out of stock keeps the whole page. Price, sizes and specs all stay, struck through where they no longer apply. The bottom bar changes job: Notify me, and Other sellers — the multi-vendor escape hatch.
Swatches stay on the leading side of the image — left in English, right in Arabic — so the thumb that scrolls the gallery is not the thumb that picks the colour.
Size boxes never mirror their content. S, M, L, XL are Latin letters in both languages; the row order flips, but the letters do not, and the row still reads small to large from the leading edge.
The spec table flips its columns, label trailing, value leading — but values that are inherently LTR (80% cotton, 480 gsm, model numbers) keep their own direction inside the cell.

Orders — decisions and RTL notes
The timeline lives inside a dark card, the same card as Home and Account. It is the one object on the screen that should feel like the app talking to you, and it makes the status readable in one glance before any scrolling.
Seven statuses, five rows. Confirmed and Processing collapse into one line, Packed and Shipped into another, because the customer cannot act on the difference. The full seven stay in the data and in the merchant view.
The current step is a ring, not a fill, and its copy is the only present-tense line on the screen — plus the one detail that answers “how long” (“4 stops before yours”).

Actions appear only when allowed. No greyed-out Cancel on a shipped order — instead a sentence saying what to do at the door.

Entry and account — decisions
Language is the splash's only job. Each option is labelled in both scripts — العربية / Arabic — so neither speaker has to recognise a foreign word to find their own. Nothing else competes for the tap.
The driver role is explained, not offered. A sentence on the role screen says drivers are added by the admin, which stops people hunting for a missing sign-up.
The sign-in error keeps the typed email and never says which of the two fields was wrong — it names the pair and offers the reset in the same breath.
Phone sign-in sits below the divider, not above. Email is primary for now; if the Iraqi user base leans on phone numbers, swapping the two is a one-line change and the layout holds.
"Open a store" is the one accented row in Account — orange tile, its own card, outside the grouped lists. It is the bridge between the two apps, and the role choice at sign-up is never a locked door.
Sign out is the only red item and it lives at the bottom of a list, with no confirmation dialog — signing back in is cheap. Deleting the account is inside Help and support, where it needs a dialog.

Browse and checkout — decisions
Active filters are chips at the top, not a number on a button. Each one is removable where it is visible. The floating Filters pill only opens the sheet; it never becomes the only evidence that filters exist.

Merchant — decisions
“Needs you today” outranks the numbers. Revenue sits in the header because it is the reason to open the app; the work queue sits first in the body because it is the reason to keep it open. The most urgent row is the only outlined card.
Every metric carries its comparison. +12% against the same 19 days last month, 3.1% against a 2% target, payout stated after commission. No number appears alone.
Confirm and Decline are not equals. Confirm is the wide filled pill, Decline is a narrow bordered one in error ink. Declining opens a reason sheet, and the card says out loud that declines count against the rating.
Stock is edited in the list with a −/+ stepper on the row. Restocking four products should not cost four screens; only a real edit opens the product form.
Out of stock and Hidden are different states. Out of stock is visible to buyers and shows a Restock button; hidden is dimmed with a struck eye and is invisible to buyers. Merchants conflate these constantly, so the list distinguishes them by treatment, not by wording alone.
RTL: the revenue sparkline keeps its LTR direction — time runs left to right in a chart in both languages — while every label, row and badge around it mirrors. Percentages keep the sign leading.
