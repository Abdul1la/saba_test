SABA — PHASE 3 · FORMS AND SHEETS
Merchant product form
The densest screen in the app, and the one that decides whether the system holds. Eleven input groups, a media tray, and a variant matrix that can reach forty rows. The strategy is
progressive disclosure by section
: the form is a stack of white cards, each one a closed question; nothing is nested more than two levels; and the variant matrix is grouped by the first option so twenty rows arrive as four groups.
Field rhythm
Label 12/600 above · input 54 · helper or error 12/400 below ·
space/lg 16
between fields,
space/xl 24
between cards. No floating labels — Arabic ascenders collide with them.
Compact fields
Inside the matrix, inputs drop to 44 high and
radius/action 14
. That is the floor — the row itself stays 48+ and the whole row is the tap target for expanding.
Required vs optional
Optional fields are marked, not required ones. On a form where nine of eleven fields are mandatory, marking the exceptions is less noise and reads faster.
Validation timing
On blur for a field the user has left, never while typing. On submit, the page scrolls to the first error and a summary card names every one.

Product form — what the density test showed
The 54px field and 24px radius survive, the card radius does not scale down.
Eleven groups at
radius/card 24
read as a stack, not as clutter, because the gap between cards (24) is larger than the gap inside them (16). That ratio is the whole trick.
Matrix rows needed a second size of input.
54 does not fit three fields on 390. A compact 44 /
radius/action 14
variant is now part of the system, restricted to table rows where the row itself is the 48 target.
Twenty rows are shown as four groups.
Collapsed groups carry a summary that is worth reading on its own — “5 sizes · 0 in stock” — so the merchant can act without expanding. The group with an error is the one that opens.
Bulk editing comes before per-row editing.
“Apply to all” sits above the groups, because a merchant with twenty variants almost always sets one price and then corrects three.
Category-dependent fields are labelled as such.
A “From category” tag and a warning that changing category replaces them — otherwise merchants lose ten minutes of typing and blame the app.
Errors are stated three times, deliberately.
A summary at the top with jump links, the section border, and the field itself. On a form this long, one of the three is always off screen.
Submitting does not block the screen with a spinner.
A progress card lists what is done, what is uploading and what is queued, and says the merchant may leave. Photo upload over a patchy connection is the slow part, not the save.
A server error marks one field, not the form.
The record is kept as a draft, the rejected SKU is named against the product that already owns it, and a one-tap alternative is offered. Every other row shows a quiet “4 of 5 saved”.
Arabic costs about 8% more vertical space
at these line heights and no field needed re-sizing — but SKUs, dimensions and counters stay LTR inside RTL rows, and the category breadcrumb flips its chevron.
What I would change before build:
the description counter should turn amber at 90%, not red at 101%, and the variant group summary should name the failing rows rather than counting them once there are more than two.

Filter sheet — decisions
The count is the button label.
“Show all 583 results” becomes “Show 214 results” as choices are made. The user commits to a number, not to a hope.
Facet counts sit beside every option
, and a zero-count option is disabled with a sentence explaining why — that is what stops the dead-end result screen from ever being reached.
Invalid input disables the apply button and says so under it.
A greyed button with no explanation is the most common form failure; the reason sits directly beneath, not at the top of a scrolled sheet.
The fix is offered as a button.
“Swap them” is one tap and it is right next to the error. Telling the user what is wrong without offering the correction is only half the job.
Loading counts does not block choosing.
Numbers arrive as small skeleton pills inside the chips they belong to; the chips stay tappable and the button says what it is waiting for.
One facet failing does not fail the sheet.
The city group is replaced in place by its own error card with its own retry, the other groups keep working, and the apply button still carries a real count.

Review writing — decisions
Everything below the stars is dimmed until a star is tapped.
Not disabled — dimmed and still readable, so the user can see what is coming and knows the cost of continuing before they commit.
The tags are generated by the rating.
Four stars asks “what was good?”; two would ask what went wrong, with a different tag set. Writing prose is the fallback, not the expectation.
The star row is 36px with 10px gaps
— well past the 44 tap floor including padding, and large enough to hit while holding a phone one-handed on the street.
Invalid means one thing only: no rating.
The message says the text is kept, because the fear at that moment is losing what was typed, not the rule that was broken.
Submission is split, and so is failure.
The review posts, the photo fails, and the screen reports both truthfully rather than rolling back a success to keep the error message simple.
The failure states the numbers
— 14.2 MB against an 8 MB limit — and offers to shrink the file rather than asking the user to go and do it. “Skip photo” is a legitimate exit, not a punishment.

Address entry

city, area, then a free-text landmark line that is required and explained
, with a pin on a map as the ground truth. The phone number is the field a driver actually depends on, so it is validated hardest.

Address entry — decisions
The landmark field is required and has the largest input on the screen.
In Iraq it carries more delivery information than any structured field, so it is sized like the important thing it is and its helper text says why.
The map is the first thing, not a decoration at the end.
Two ways in — use my location, or drop a pin — and the empty state says plainly that drivers read the pin before the words.
Area is disabled until a city is chosen
and says so in the field, rather than presenting an empty dropdown that appears broken.
The phone is LTR in both languages
, split as +964 · number with a divider, validated on digit count, and the error names the rule (“10 digits after +964. One is missing.”) instead of saying “invalid”.
“Too vague” is a real validation.
A landmark of three words gets pushed back with an example of what would work — the alternative is a driver phoning the customer, which costs far more than a second of friction here.
The server error keeps the record.
An uncovered area does not discard the address: it saves as pickup-only, offers three covered neighbours as one-tap swaps, and ticks the fields that did pass so the user sees what survived.
